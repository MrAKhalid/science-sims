Impulse

Impulse = FΔt = Δp

Momentum vectors

pf = pi + Δp  (Δp points the same way as F)

From a given height, every drop ends the same way: the egg goes from moving to stopped, so its change in momentum is the same. What changes is how long the stop takes. A soft surface makes the stop last longer, so the force on the egg is smaller.

Landing surface

Same impulse, different force

Key idea

Favg = ΔpΔt

From a given height, the egg needs the same impulse to stop no matter what it lands on. A big force for a short time or a small force for a long time can give the same impulse. Stretch out the time and the force goes down. The same idea is behind air bags, crumple zones, helmets and bending your knees when you land.

A golfer can only swing so hard, but they can change how long the club stays on the ball. A full follow-through and a springy club face keep them in contact longer. More contact time means a bigger impulse, so the ball leaves with more momentum and flies farther.

Key idea

FΔt = Δp
= mvf − mvi

The ball starts at rest, so its final momentum equals the impulse from the club. With the force capped by how hard you can swing, the way to a bigger impulse is a longer contact time. That is why coaches teach follow-through in golf, tennis, baseball and hockey, and why a flexing stick or a springy club face helps.

Contact times are about half a millisecond. Real follow-through only stretches them a little, so the differences here are exaggerated to make them easy to see. Carry distance uses a launch-monitor rule of thumb (about 3.1 m of carry per m/s of ball speed).
