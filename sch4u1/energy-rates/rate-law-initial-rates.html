How is the rate being monitored?

Chemists follow this reaction with a spectrophotometer, which measures how much light a solution absorbs.

  • The product, hypoiodite (OI−), absorbs ultraviolet light at about 292 nm. OCl− and I− barely absorb at that wavelength.
  • As OI− forms, less light gets through to the detector, so the absorbance rises.
  • Absorbance is directly proportional to [OI−], so the computer turns each reading into a concentration and plots the graph you see.
  • The real reaction is very fast, so chemists use a stopped-flow mixer that mixes the two solutions and starts measuring within milliseconds.
Think about it: why wouldn't a conductivity meter work for this reaction?
Two ions react and two ions form (OCl− + I− → OI− + Cl−), so the number of ions in solution, and therefore the conductivity, hardly changes.
